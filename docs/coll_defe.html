Collective defense strategies

Isolates selection

Rationale

The first phase of this study aimed to select an entomopathogenic fungal isolate to evaluate how a toxic substrate affects the collective immune defense of Atta sexdens.

Pathogenicity was evaluated on A. sexdens foragers exposed to five Metarhizium robertsii isolates—kindly provided by the Laboratório de Interações Inseto-Microrganismos at the Universidade Federal de Viçosa.

Twenty-four hours prior to the bioassays, colonies were starved. Following starvation, colonies were randomly assigned to two groups and exposed to one of two substrate regimes for seven days: (i) 10.0 g of Acalypha wilkesiana leaves daily (control group), or (ii) 10.0 g of fresh Tropaeolum majus leaves daily, serving as a potential modulating agent of social defenses.

After this period, individual ants from these colonies were immersed for two seconds in a conidial suspension at a concentration of 1x10⁶ conidia/mL, containing distilled water and 0.01% Tween 80. Three control groups were evaluated: (1) an aqueous solution of 0.01% Tween 80 (vehicle control), (2) distilled water (negative control), and (3) a 0.1% isocycloseram solution (positive control). Each treatment consisted of four replicates (Petri dishes) with five foragers each. Ants were housed in Petri dishes lined with moistened filter paper and maintained in a BOD incubator at 27 ± 2 °C, 70 ± 10% relative humidity, and continuous darkness (0:24 h L:D). The ants were starved throughout the 5-day evaluation period, and mortality was recorded daily. Dead individuals were immediately removed and transferred to individual moist chambers (microcentrifuge tubes containing cotton and autoclaved distilled water) to evaluate fungal sporulation (confirming mycosis).

To ensure that colonies actively interacted with the provided substrates and to characterize potential sublethal behavioral effects during the 7-day pre-exposure period, daily foraging activity (leaf consumption) and waste production were monitored for each colony. Quantifying consumption confirmed whether T. majus induced feeding deterrence over time while maintaining active substrate intake, whereas waste accumulation served as a baseline indicator of colony maintenance and hygienic activity prior to pathogen exposure.

Inferencial statistics

Foraging activity (substrate consumption) and waste production data were analyzed using mixed-effects modeling frameworks in R. Experimental day was zero-indexed by subtracting 1 from the original day variable (day_c = day - 1), setting Day 1 as time zero to facilitate the interpretation of model intercepts and initial treatment effects. Model assumptions were evaluated through visual inspection of raw and simulated quantile residuals using the DHARMa package, as well as Shapiro–Wilk tests for residual normality.

Foraging consumption data exhibited strong heteroscedasticity and non-normality when fitted with standard linear mixed models. Therefore, consumption was analyzed using a Generalized Linear Mixed Model (GLMM) via the glmmTMB package, specifying a Gamma distribution with a log-link function and a substrate-specific dispersion structure (dispformula = ~ substrate). Waste production met linear mixed model assumptions and was analyzed using a standard Linear Mixed Model (LMM) fitted with the lmer function from the lme4 package. Both models included substrate, day (day_c), and their interaction as fixed effects, and colony as a random intercept to account for the non-independence of repeated observations from the same colony.

Hypothesis testing was performed using Type III Wald \(\chi^2\) tests via the car package. Estimated marginal means were obtained using the emmeans package to characterize predicted values across experimental days, while emtrends was used to compare the temporal slopes of consumption between substrates.

Foraging activity differed between substrates and changed differently over time. A generalized linear mixed-effects model (GLMM) with a Gamma distribution and substrate-specific dispersion structure revealed a significant main effect of substrate on consumption (Type III Wald \(\chi^2\) test, \(\chi^2_1 = 20.48\), \(P < 0.001\)) and a significant substrate × day interaction (\(\chi^2_1 = 6.40\), \(P = 0.0114\)), whereas the main effect of day was not significant (\(\chi^2_1 = 0.0076\), \(P = 0.931\)). Estimated marginal trends indicated that consumption remained stable over time on the control substrate (\(\text{slope} = 0.0002\) log-units/day, \(SE = 0.0024\), \(P > 0.05\)), whereas it decreased significantly over time on Tropaeolum majus (\(\text{slope} = -0.1247\) log-units/day, \(SE = 0.0493\), \(P < 0.05\)). Accordingly, temporal slopes differed significantly between substrates (\(z = 2.53\), \(P = 0.0114\)). Model diagnostics using simulated quantile residuals indicated no evidence of residual deviation, overdispersion, or outliers (Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, \(P = 0.486\)).

Survival curves (Kaplan-Meier)

Survival data were evaluated using time-to-event analysis in R. Prior to inferential modeling, the proportional hazards assumption was assessed for all covariates using Schoenfeld residual diagnostics (cox.zph function in the survival package). The chemical positive control (isocycloseram) induced complete mortality within 48 h, causing a significant violation of proportional hazards (\(\chi^2 = 35.43, P < 0.001\)). Because this treatment served strictly as a technical validation control, it was excluded from further inferential modeling to ensure statistical validity. In the remaining dataset comprising fungal isolates and negative controls, proportional hazards assumptions were fully met (global Schoenfeld test, \(\chi^2 = 10.34, P = 0.67\)).

Differences in survival rates were analyzed using a Mixed-Effects Cox Proportional Hazards model (coxme package). The model included substrate regime, fungal treatment, and their interaction (substrate * treatment) as fixed effects. Colony of origin was included as a random intercept (1 | colony) to account for potential non-independence among nestmates. Overall treatment effects were evaluated using Type III Wald \(\chi^2\) tests (car package). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons of hazard ratios (HR) were performed using the emmeans package, applying Tukey’s adjustment for multiple comparisons within and across substrate regimes.

Survival of A. sexdens foragers was significantly influenced by both the fungal isolate (Type III Wald \(\chi^2\) test: \(\chi^2_6 = 15.18, P = 0.0189\)) and the pre-exposure substrate regime (\(\chi^2_1 = 8.95, P = 0.0028\)). The substrate \(\times\) treatment interaction was not statistically significant (\(\chi^2_6 = 8.05, P = 0.2343\)).

Pre-exposure to T. majus significantly reduced baseline worker survival compared to the control substrate (A. wilkesiana). In non-inoculated controls, ants exposed to T. majus exhibited a 4.25-fold increase in mortality hazard relative to those maintained on the control diet (\(z = 2.99, P = 0.0028\)).

Under standard control substrate conditions, isolate 15 was the most virulent, causing a significant 4.31-fold increase in mortality hazard compared to non-inoculated controls (\(z = 3.04, P = 0.0377\)). Isolate 94 also displayed elevated hazard rates under control conditions, though this trend was marginal (\(HR = 3.89, z = 2.80, P = 0.0752\)).

When fungal exposure was combined with T. majus pre-exposure, mortality hazards increased overall. Isolate 33 exhibited a high absolute hazard rate on the T. majus substrate, with worker hazard increasing 3.37-fold compared to the same isolate on the control substrate (\(z = 3.42, P = 0.0006\)). Pre-exposure to T. majus also heightened mortality hazards for isolates 124 (\(HR = 2.68, P = 0.0184\)) and 94 (\(HR = 2.00, P = 0.0492\)). Conversely, isolate 15 maintained consistently high virulence across diets (\(HR = 1.12, P = 0.7497\)), demonstrating high intrinsic pathogenicity regardless of substrate-induced stress.

Median lethal time (\(LT_{50}\)) estimates derived from Kaplan–Meier survival curves further highlighted the differential speed of kill among treatment combinations. The chemical positive control (isocycloseram) demonstrated the fastest mortality, reaching 50% mortality within 1.0 day regardless of substrate regime (\(95\%\text{ CI: } 1.0–1.0\)). In non-inoculated negative controls, worker survival remained high on the control diet (A. wilkesiana), with mortality failing to reach 50% during the observation period (\(LT_{50} = \text{Not Reached, NR}\)). In contrast, pre-exposure to T. majus alone markedly reduced survival, resulting in an \(LT_{50}\) of 3.0 days (\(95\%\text{ CI: } 2.0–\text{NR}\)).

Under control substrate conditions (A. wilkesiana), fungal isolate 15 exhibited the shortest median lethal time among biological treatments (\(LT_{50} = 3.5\text{ days}, 95\%\text{ CI: } 3.0–5.0\)), followed by 94 (\(LT_{50} = 4.0\text{ days}, 95\%\text{ CI: } 2.0–\text{NR}\)). When fungal exposure was combined with T. majus pre-exposure, speed of kill was further accelerated; isolate 33 achieved the overall shortest median lethal time (\(LT_{50} = 2.0\text{ days}, 95\%\text{ CI: } 2.0–4.0\)), followed closely by 94 (\(LT_{50} = 2.5\text{ days}, 95\%\text{ CI: } 2.0–4.0\)) and 15 (\(LT_{50} = 3.0\text{ days}, 95\%\text{ CI: } 2.0–5.0\)).
